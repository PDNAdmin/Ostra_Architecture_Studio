How do you design a house for daylight?

By Rohan Mehta, Partner, Design Director. Updated 15 July 2026.

In this article
  1. Start with where the sun goes
  2. Window position matters more than window size
  3. Keep rooms shallow enough for light to reach the back
  4. Plan for summer as well as winter
  5. What to do next

Rooms that are too dark are one of the most common regrets in a new home. Bigger windows alone do not fix it, and they can make rooms too hot in summer.

Start with where the sun goes

In the UK, south light is warm and changes through the day, north light is cool and even, and low east and west sun reaches deep into a room. We place rooms by when you use them: breakfast where the morning sun lands, a study where the light stays steady.

Window position matters more than window size

A tall window lets light reach further into a room than a wide one of the same area. Light from two sides makes a room feel bright and even. A roof light gives far more light than a wall window of the same size.

Keep rooms shallow enough for light to reach the back

As a rule of thumb, daylight reaches about twice the height of the window head into a room. Deeper than that, the back of the room needs light from another side or from above.

Plan for summer as well as winter

The same sun that warms a room in winter can overheat it in July. Overhangs, deep reveals and shading let the low winter sun in and keep the high summer sun out.

What to do next

Tell us how you use each room through the day. We model sunlight on your site at every season before we fix a single window. See a garden studio lit from the north, so the light stays even all day, at Ashdown Studio.
