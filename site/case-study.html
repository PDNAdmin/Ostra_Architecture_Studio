The brief

Hannah and Joel Pryce came to us with a dark 1960s bungalow on a plot with a long view south to the Downs that turned its back on the sun, with a busy lane along the north side.

The challenge

The plot sits on the edge of the Lewes conservation area, and planners wanted the new house no taller than the bungalow it replaced. The lane to the north is noisy, and the neighbours to the east look straight down into the garden. A tall, open house was never going to be allowed, or comfortable.

What we did

  1. Design

    We wrapped the house around a court. The two-storey wing shelters the court from the lane, and every room opens onto it, so each one gets the sun at some point in the day. From the street it is quiet and low; from inside, the court is the centre of everything.

  2. Materials

    Lime render breathes and softens with age. Handmade Sussex clay tiles match the roofs around it. Oak frames and lime plaster inside keep the rooms warm in colour and easy to repair, so the house feels settled rather than new.

  3. Build

    Downland Build started on site in May 2024. We held a site meeting every week and sent the Pryces a short report each Friday, with photos and the cost to date. One change, a larger kitchen roof light, was priced and agreed before it was built.
